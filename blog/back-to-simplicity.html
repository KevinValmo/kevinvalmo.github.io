Back to Simplicity

Why I no longer use frameworks, libraries and development tools for this blog.

html css simplicity web
⏳ 3 min read  |  Apr 10, 2026

The Usual Suspects

Angular, React, Analog.js, Blazor, Tailwind... these are some of the most well-known frameworks and tools I have worked with over the past few years to build websites. They are all great. They all have their pros and cons. And they have all helped me ship things faster, with less code and with a more polished result.

But it is easy to overcomplicate things. Sometimes that complexity is necessary. And sometimes it is just nice to go back to the basics — back to the foundation of the web, to pure HTML and CSS.

The Question I Asked Myself

At some point I stopped and thought: why am I making my life harder by pulling in frameworks and libraries for a personal blog? Would it not be simpler to write a bit of HTML and CSS and just publish my thoughts — without worrying about configuring packages, updating dependencies, chasing down bugs introduced by a minor version bump, and everything else that comes with a modern frontend setup?

The answer, it turned out, was yes. It really would be simpler.

Thanks to tools like AI, I do not even need a framework to get things done quickly. I do not need a markdown-to-HTML parser, I do not need a build pipeline, I do not need a local dev server. I just write and ask the AI to help me shape the HTML and CSS. That is what I did here, and it works.

The Right Tool for the Right Job

Years of experience in software development have taught me that there is always a balance to strike — and that balance is always relative to the context you are in. The golden rule I keep coming back to is: use the right tool for the right job.

For a large product, a complex application, or a team of developers who need consistency and speed, a framework like Angular or React makes complete sense. For a personal blog with a handful of static pages? Pure HTML and CSS is the right tool.

I could have gone down the platform route — Wordpress, Ghost, Medium, Substack. They are all fine options. But I did not want that. I want full control over my blog. I want to be able to change every single detail. I want to write my own code without having to work around the constraints of whatever tool I happened to choose.

So Here We Are

A simple blog. No dependencies. No complications. No build step. Just HTML and CSS, nothing more.

It loads fast, it is easy to maintain, and every change I make is a change I fully understand. When something looks wrong, I open the stylesheet and fix it. There is no abstraction layer in the way. There is no framework to blame.

Sometimes the best engineering decision is to remove things rather than add them. This was one of those times.
